## Structure

1. **The 11 p.m. problem.** Every small owner knows they should reply
   to every review, fast and gracefully — and does it at midnight,
   tired, either too sharp or too grovelling. The subscription tools
   fix the writing and take the data: every complaint, every customer
   name, on someone else's server, forever.
2. **The pitch in one line.** Same job, running entirely on the
   owner's laptop: Gemma 3 (4B, via Ollama) drafts, a validator
   checks the draft like an owner would, one coached retry if it
   misses, templates if it still misses. No subscription, no API
   bill, nothing leaves the machine.
3. **What "a good reply" actually is.** The validator's rules —
   thank first, name the business, answer a *specific*, apologise
   and offer something concrete when it's unhappy, invite them back.
   Two war stories from building it:
   - the signature cheat — the first "generic reply" test passed its
     specifics check only because the sign-off ("– The Moe's Coffee
     team") contained the word *coffee*. The checker now strips the
     sign-off before looking.
   - "that's on us" ≠ "this one's on us" — an apology idiom tripping
     the offer detector; the check now looks for the treat, not the
     apology wearing its clothes.
   - the typography false alarm — Gemma wrote Moe's with a curly
     apostrophe and the name check failed a reply that named the
     business three times; and its coached retry, told to drop a
     bracketed placeholder, invented a plausible owner email
     instead. Both now have rules and regression tests; "a
     complimentary upgrade" is recognised as the offer it is.
4. **Live runs.** The three examples (glowing, angry, mixed): the
   review, the reply, which engine wrote it, whether a retry fired.
   Quote the actual credit lines — honesty about the engine is part
   of the product.
5. **What I'd sell, and to whom.** The free tool as-is is a weekend
   build; the sellable version is the same engine inside the places
   owners already live (a browser button on the review dashboard).
   The privacy angle is the moat a cloud tool can't copy without
   ceasing to be a cloud tool.
6. **Honest limits.** Form, not facts; sarcasm beats the sentiment
   heuristic; a human should still read before posting.
