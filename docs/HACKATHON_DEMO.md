# Submission demonstration and first-operator trial

## Recording outline

Record the actual running browser app. Use fictional examples unless a business
operator has consented to share their data. Do not display local session links,
private profiles or customer identifiers in the recording.

1. Explain the job: an owner needs a usable review reply, not an AI-generated
   refund policy. Show the local browser workspace and the owner-policy field.
2. Select Local Gemma 3 (4B). Use the complaint example and show the 25-minute
   wait and unanswered question. Record the actual outcome. Keep a fallback's
   label visible; do not relabel it as model output. Disclose any edit or time cut.
3. Show Details to preserve. Remove the wait duration, save and recheck, and show
   that approval is blocked. Restore the detail and recheck.
4. Add an unauthorized refund sentence, save, and show the policy finding. Remove
   it. Read and approve the exact saved draft, then copy it for manual posting.
5. Open the mixed accommodation example. Show both check-in and shower concerns
   in the response. Briefly show Quality Lab's evidence and unknown human scores.
6. Close with the limitation: the tool assists a human, does not post automatically,
   and has not proved every statement correct. Supply the public code link.

A roughly two-minute edited recording is a useful target, not a promise about
model latency. A recorded replay or template demonstration must say so on screen.

## First operator trial

Ask one consenting cafe owner, shop operator or accommodation host to try five
reviews of their own choosing. Do not preselect only reviews that produce good
outputs. The operator, not an automated test, should make the final assessments.

Record their role, whether the reviews were fictional or real, and whether they
consented to anonymized quotes. For each review record engine, retries, blocking
findings, editing time, corrections, and usable/needs editing/unusable. Ask which
workflow step was confusing and whether they would use it again. Ask about price
only after they have tried it; a proposed price is not willingness-to-pay evidence.

Do not manufacture a testimonial or count yourself as the owner's first customer.
A public review of a real business is a test input, not a business partnership.
